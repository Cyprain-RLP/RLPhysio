In plain English

What is a musculoskeletal condition?

It is any problem with the parts of the body that help you move: bones, joints, muscles, tendons and ligaments. Back pain, arthritis, sprains and long-standing aches are all common examples. Most of our work centres on helping people with these conditions.

Workstation and ergonomic assessmentsDesk set-up, posture and manual handling

We check desk set-up, seating and posture, and look at manual handling tasks, to find where strain is building up and how to reduce it.

Research on workplace equipment changes alone shows little benefit for people who already have arm, neck or shoulder pain, and exercise appears to work better.2 So our recommendations pair practical set-up changes with movement and strength advice.

Treatment and rehabilitationHands-on therapy and exercise to get people back to full duties

Hands-on therapy, massage, cupping and taping, alongside rehabilitation exercise, to help people move more comfortably and get back to full duties.

Acupuncture is also available. It may give temporary relief from some types of long-term pain, and NICE guidance says a course can be considered for chronic primary pain.3

Return-to-work supportPhased returns, modified duties and workplace adjustments

Practical advice after illness or injury, including phased returns, modified duties and workplace adjustments, tailored to the person and the job.

Official fit note guidance lists phased return, altered hours, amended duties and workplace adaptations as the options a healthcare professional can suggest, and says work can help recovery and wellbeing.4

Supporting teams under pressureThe physical side of work stress

Burnout is described by the World Health Organization as the result of workplace stress that has not been successfully managed, and is classed as an occupational phenomenon rather than a medical condition.5

We do not diagnose or treat burnout. We help with the physical side of work stress, such as muscle tension, aches and inactivity, and work with employers on realistic habits for movement and recovery.

Mindfulness training is one approach with research behind it. A meta-analysis of 38 randomised trials in healthcare staff found moderate reductions in stress, anxiety and depression, and a small reduction in burnout.6 Those studies involved healthcare workers, so results may differ in other workplaces. BreathworkSimple breathing techniques and the research behind them
In plain English

Breathwork means deliberate, structured breathing practices, such as slowing your breathing and making the breath out longer than the breath in. They take a few minutes and need no equipment, so they can be done at a desk.

Guided sessions

We lead short, relaxed sessions that teach simple breathing techniques and explain how stress shows up in the body.

Desk-based techniques

Quick practices staff can use between meetings or at their workstation, taking a few minutes at a time.

For teams

Group sessions for your workplace, offered alongside movement and ergonomic advice as part of looking after the physical side of work stress.

The research

What the evidence says

Breathwork compared with control groups

A meta-analysis of randomised trials found that breathwork was linked to lower self-reported stress, anxiety and depression than control conditions. Most of the stress trials used slow-paced breathing.

Daily practice

5 minutesa day for 28 days, 108 adults

Short daily breathing practices and mood

In a randomised study, adults did one of three breathing practices or mindfulness meditation for five minutes a day, at home. Anxiety and negative mood fell in every group. Breathing rate fell more with the breathing practices, and cyclic sighing gave the largest gain in positive mood.

cyclic sighing rose more than mindfulness

Cyclic sighing is two breaths in followed by one long breath out.

Balban et al., 2023, Cell Reports Medicine2

How to read these studies
  • Most trials in the Fincham review had a moderate risk of bias, the studies were small and the outcomes were self-reported. The authors urge caution and call for better-designed research.
  • The Balban study was exploratory and ran remotely for four weeks, mostly with university students. The comparison group did mindfulness rather than nothing, so it shows how breathing practices compare, not whether they beat doing nothing.
  • The figures are group averages. Some people benefit a lot and others very little.
  • Breathing practices are not a treatment for any condition and may not suit everyone, for example some people with heart or breathing conditions. Tell us about your health first.

Mindfulness for pain and stressPractical sessions and the research behind them
In plain English

Instead of fighting or ignoring what you feel, you learn to notice it calmly and decide how to respond. The best-studied programme is mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR), usually eight weekly group sessions of around two hours plus practice at home.

Mindfulness for persistent pain

Guided practices in breathing, body awareness and gentle movement that help you notice pain and respond to it differently. Used alongside exercise and treatment, never instead of medical care.

Stress and tension

Short practices for the physical side of stress, such as tense muscles and shallow breathing, that you can use at home or at your desk.

Mindfulness for teams

Introductory sessions for staff in your workplace, built around how stress shows up in the body.

The research

What the evidence says

A network meta-analysis pooled 21 randomised trials in adults with chronic pain. It compared group-based MBSR and cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT, a talking therapy often used for chronic pain) with control conditions, looking at physical function, pain intensity and depression symptoms.

How to read these studies
  • They tested structured programmes, typically eight weekly group sessions plus practice at home, led by trained teachers. They do not show that every form of mindfulness helps.
  • The figures are group averages. Some people improved a lot and others very little.
  • In the Khoo review only one trial compared MBSR with CBT directly, so that comparison rests mostly on indirect evidence.
  • The Spinelli studies involved healthcare staff and the Kuyken studies involved people with recurrent depression. We do not diagnose or treat depression, and mindfulness is not a substitute for medical care.

Community

Physiotherapy in your own home

Whether you are recovering from surgery or illness, or living with a long-term condition, we bring assessment, treatment and rehabilitation to you.

Serving North London and surrounding areas

In plain English

A musculoskeletal condition is a problem with your bones, joints, muscles, tendons or ligaments, such as back pain, arthritis or a sprain. Much of our community work is helping people manage these conditions and stay active.

Treatment in your own home

Appointments take place where you live, so there is no travelling when you are in pain, recovering or finding it hard to get out.

Functional assessment

We look at how you manage everyday movements such as getting up from a chair, walking, using stairs and reaching, then work out what is getting in the way.

Rehabilitation after illness or surgery

Gradual, structured recovery after an illness, hospital stay or operation, including post-operative rehabilitation, so you rebuild strength and confidence at a pace that suits you.

Long-term condition management

Ongoing support for conditions such as arthritis, hypermobility and persistent pain, with day-to-day strategies and safe, adapted exercise.

Balance assessment

We check your balance and how you walk, and set up exercises to help you stay strong and steady. NICE guidance on preventing falls includes balance and gait checks and helping people keep their strength and balance through activity and simple exercises.1

Tailored exercise programmes

Exercise plans built around your condition, goals and abilities. NICE recommends exercise, and staying physically active, as part of managing chronic primary pain.2

Mindfulness for pain

Guided practices that help you notice and respond differently to persistent pain, used alongside your exercises and treatment. In a trial of 342 adults with chronic low back pain, 61% of those who took an eight-week mindfulness programme had a meaningful improvement in function at 26 weeks, compared with 44% who had usual care.3

Acupuncture

May give temporary relief from some types of long-term pain. NICE guidance says a course of acupuncture or dry needling can be considered for chronic primary pain.2

Massage

Massage can form part of your treatment plan alongside exercise and rehabilitation.

What to expect

  1. Assessment

    We go through your history, test movement and strength, and work out what is behind the problem.

  2. Plan

    We agree goals with you and start treatment, with advice and exercises you can follow between visits.

  3. Progress

    Follow-up visits track how you are doing and adjust the plan as you improve.

Personal training

Training that starts with assessment

Before we build a fitness plan, we look closely at how you move, what hurts and what your body has been through. Your programme is built by our physiotherapy team.

Before we train

What we check first

Movement screening

How you squat, bend, reach, step and balance, to spot limits and imbalances before they become problems.

Mobility and function

How freely your joints move and how well you manage the everyday and sporting tasks you want to do.

Pain triggers and past injuries

What brings on discomfort, what you have injured before and how that shapes what is sensible now.

Medical history

Conditions, surgery and medication that affect how hard and how fast we progress.

Exercise technique

A careful look at how you perform each exercise, with coaching and adjustments as you get stronger.

Bridging rehab to fitness

Many people finish rehab but are not ready for a normal gym class. We close that gap, taking you step by step from recovery after surgery, illness or a long period of inactivity towards better strength, function and fitness.

Move on from post-operative rehabilitation to building strength and fitness.

After illness

Rebuild stamina and confidence at a pace your body can handle.

After time away from exercise

Restart safely if you have lost fitness, are carrying an old injury or are new to training.

About

Our team

R&L Physio is a physiotherapy and personal training practice serving people in North London and businesses across London.

We combine clinical physiotherapy with training expertise. Our work centres on musculoskeletal conditions, post-operative assessment and care, and bringing people back to strength and fitness.

Every physiotherapist in our team is registered with the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC), the regulator that protects the title of physiotherapist in the UK.

In the community we treat people in their own homes. In the workplace we work as occupational physiotherapists, helping employers look after the physical health of their people.
